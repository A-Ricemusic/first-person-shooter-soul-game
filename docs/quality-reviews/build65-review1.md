Played exactly two normal attempts through the visible driver, using only its help, screenshots and exposed UI. No source, hidden state, tests, prior reports, arranged actors or resources used. Retained action protocol and photographs: `docs/media/build65-review1/`. Browser timed synthetic controls with frequent Escape pauses. Software WebGL fallback. These observations do not certify human enjoyment, audio quality, hardware performance or AAA production readiness.

Ratings use 0–10: 0 unusable/absent, 3 substantial shortcomings, 5 functional but mixed, 7 strong with noticeable gaps, 9 excellent shipping quality, 10 exceptional. G means graphics and visual craft; P means play systems and tactical experience; U means usability and onboarding. Independent judgments, limited to observed slice:

- G 6: coherent rainy industrial setting, warm/cool lighting contrast, attractive title and end screens; the Heart is a useful landmark. Close robot and coil geometry is quite simple, with broad flat surfaces and repeated storage shapes. Visually competent stylized prototype, below contemporary AAA environmental richness.
- P 5: precise optic kills and meaningful side approaches worked. Recovery rewards and changing objectives produced understandable progress. Stationary inaccurate shooting was harshly punished. Yet most memories were recovered while bypassing combat, and I have not established that combat is satisfying in human continuous play. Coil fight created a clear escalation but my failures included targeting mistakes and exposure, so difficulty conclusions remain tentative.
- U 7: strong objective labels, distance markers, recovery reach feedback, map, trace, retry and terminal summary. First friction was turning written advice into an optic aim: the initial center crosshair sat below the optic. Afterward my aim repeatedly missed moving sentinels, and I spent two long E holds outside recovery reach. The exact outside-reach distance explained the correction. Dense small text and simultaneous center/top/side overlays occasionally competed with target visibility.
- Readability 6: cyan navigation versus amber threats generally helps. Small sentinels against cover and amber coils behind shutters were harder to resolve. At close range a shutter filled the sight and a magazine produced no observed additional coil progress. The UI named armored/open states clearly, but identifying a clean shot to remaining components while under fire was harder.
- AAA readiness 3: this evidence supports an attractive functional vertical slice, not a full AAA claim. Simple geometry, limited observed enemy variety and target feedback, plus untested breadth, audio, continuous human control and hardware leave a large certification gap. This is an evidence-limited judgment, not a claim of unseen defects.
